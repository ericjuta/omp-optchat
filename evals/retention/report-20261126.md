## Reading

- No answer in the six cells was graded `fabricated-citation` or ungrounded. Every wrong answer (3 at 128,000 bytes, 1 at 64,000) is the ambiguous question, which the whole-history arm also missed in two of three seeds.
- The first run's loss at 512 records is consistent with the runner's grading and not with the view, but this run does not prove it: the answers are new samples, and the old artifacts cannot be regraded. The model reads original text through `chat_search` in more than half of its turns, and 13 correct answers here used no `zoom` at all; the first runner would have failed those.
- The view arms are new samples, not a regrade, so sampling can also move a score. The first run's `fabricated-citation` grades went from 21 to 0, which sampling does not explain.
- Decision rule: the 64,000-byte arm has 2 more correct answers than the 128,000-byte arm (at most 2 fewer are allowed), and its main-model cost is 23.9% lower (at least 15% is required). By the preregistered rule a 64,000-byte view is worth a check on real chats. This reverses the first report's "closed". No default changes from this run.
- With the default view the view arm still cost more than whole history ($4.02 against $3.53). The 64,000-byte arm cost less ($3.33).

## Limits

- Synthetic corpus, memory-only arms, one main model and one compressor. Nothing here measures real chats, prefetch or `chat_recall`.
- 72 questions per cell from 3 corpora; questions within a corpus are not independent. Neither sign test shows a difference; the run cannot tell a small one from none.
- A `chat_search` snippet is the record's text on one line, with its whitespace flattened. A quote copied from a snippet is accepted as the tool showed it, which can differ in whitespace from the stored record.
- The whole-history cells ran on an earlier commit than the rerun cells; `src/` is the same.
- The 512-record corpus fits the main model's context. The run says nothing about chats longer than that.
