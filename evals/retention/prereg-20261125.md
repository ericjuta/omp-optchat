# Preregistration: retention by chat length, and a 64,000-byte view (2026-11-25)

Written before any paid call. Changing anything below after the first paid call needs a new file.

## Questions

1. **Retention.** How many of the answers a model gets right with the whole history in context does it still get right with OptChat's view and memory tools? Reported by chat length.
2. **View size.** Does a 64,000-byte view answer as well as the default 128,000-byte view, and what does it cost per run?

The idea of reporting retention against a whole-context baseline, by length, comes from Ramp Labs' speculative-encoding results (https://github.com/b10-tv/speculative-encoding).

## Setup

- Runner: `bun run evidence:recall` at the commit that holds this file, on the installed OMP. Driver: `evals/retention/run.sh`. Report: `bun run evidence:retention`.
- Main model `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5` (catalog window 1,000,000 tokens, so the off arm holds every record). Compressor `anthropic/claude-haiku-5-5`.
- Corpus: synthetic, 6 projects, 1,024-byte records, adversarial question emphasis, 24 of the 40 questions, one question per turn.
- Chat lengths: 48, 128 and 512 records (about 51 KB, 137 KB and 548 KB of record text).
- Arms per corpus: `off` (plain OMP, whole history in context) and `on` at `--view-bytes 128000`. At 512 records also `on` at `--view-bytes 64000`. At 48 and 128 records every summary line fits both budgets, so the two views would be the same.
- Seeds: 1, then 2, then 3, as far as the budget reaches. Every cell of a seed runs before the next seed starts. Results do not decide whether a later seed runs.

## Budget

- Total hard cap: **$40**, approved by Eric on 2026-11-25. The driver enforces it: a cell starts only when its whole worst-case cap fits what is left.
- Cell caps (worst case, not expected spend): off arm $12, $5 and $3 at 512, 128 and 48 records; every on arm $8.
- Expected spend is about $7 to $15 per seed. This is an estimate from the 0.5.0 measurements, not a measurement.
- A cell that fails, stops on its cap or leaves no artifact ends the run. Unobserved spend ends the run.

## What is reported

- Per chat length: correct answers in each arm, retention (view correct over whole-history correct, on questions judged in both), the same ratio for correct values, the per-question split, an exact two-sided sign test, and each arm's cost.
- At 512 records: the same for 128,000 against 64,000 bytes, with main prompt tokens and view lines.
- Every cell that ran is reported, including stopped ones. An off arm that compacted is not a baseline and is left out of retention, by name.

## Decision rules

- Retention is a measurement. It has no pass mark and changes nothing.
- No default changes from this run. If the 64,000-byte arm has at most 2 fewer correct answers than the 128,000-byte arm over the pooled questions, and its main-model cost is at least 15% lower, the next step is a check on real chats, proposed to Eric. Otherwise the 128,000-byte default stands and the question is closed.

## Limits, known before the run

- The corpus is synthetic: planted facts with distinctive names. Earlier runs on it scored 24/24 in both arms at about 74,000 tokens, so a result near 100% is likely and says little about real chats.
- Both arms run memory-only: no rules, prefetch, value hints or `chat_recall`. The runner cannot observe judge spend, so it cannot admit those calls under a dollar cap. A smaller view plus prefetch is therefore not tested here.
- A corpus has at most 512 records, so the 128,000-byte view merges about half of its lines once. Live chats run to thousands of messages and merge far deeper.
- Each view budget builds its own summary tree, so the two view arms differ in compressor draws as well as in budget.
- 24 questions per cell and seed. With two seeds a cell has 48 paired questions, which can show only large differences.
- A sign test that finds no difference does not show the arms are equal.
